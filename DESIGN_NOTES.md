# Original Populous influences and current direction

The original *Populous* links level land to settlement, settlement to population, and population to more power over the land. Its high-level influence over autonomous followers is the central reference for this game. *Populous II* broadens the terrain powers. *Populous: The Beginning* provides a useful 3D sense of the shaman, followers, landmarks, and spells. The bundled [original game manual](populous.pdf) remains a reference for terrain and settlement behaviour.

The Living Faith uses that land-and-followers loop for a contest of devotion. A level 2×2 patch creates room for a hut; level neighbours, household growth and building age grow it through a house, fort and castle at separate milestones, with larger foundations claimed by the building. The central design rule is now: **persistent player actions change the map or environment, not follower priorities.** Settlers claim useful ground automatically. Groves create timber and hunting opportunities, exposed mineral seams attract miners, open distant land encourages scouting, and shaped sacred terraces attract worship or defence. The player chooses where to shape the world and where to send the shaman; followers decide how to respond. Owned stones have visible spiritual health and can change hands under shaman pressure. Faith buys environmental changes; productive sites earn Devotion.

The 64×48 world spaces the starting villages across a central crossing and offers five sacred sites on upper, lower and middle routes. Mirrored peaks, surrounding hills, two connected rivers and their lakes are guaranteed on every seed; the centre routes stay open. A minimap keeps the territory navigable. Terrain and foliage use instanced meshes, while land shaping updates only the changed tile's terrain slots. This retains the single-tile land decision as the world grows.

The match now asks for 4,000 Devotion and three distinct regional festivals. A tribe can prepare each region through a seven-tile stone terrace or a mature blessed home near its rune. North and South homes need to be houses; the Crossing needs a fort. The opponent follows the same victory rule. This keeps the large map relevant after the initial economy starts scoring. The objective bar tracks completion without choosing actions for the player. The opening field guide shows a capture of the generated Three.js world and has tabs for the rules; the old floating action suggestions and step-by-step tutorial are removed.

Future design work should be driven by human playtests. Candidate improvements are clearer path previews on difficult terrain, stronger visual feedback as villages expand, richer terrain-specific choices around each stone, and a responsive rival without perfect information. Avoid labour-management menus, build-focus toggles, or extra currencies just to solve a pacing issue; if the player needs more influence, prefer a new environmental affordance whose consequences followers can read from the world.
